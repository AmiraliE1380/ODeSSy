\begin{abstract}
Aggressive compiler optimization today occupies two distant extremes.
Conventional optimizers run in milliseconds but reason through ad-hoc
heuristics that miss opportunities; superoptimizers synthesize faster code
but take hours, confining them to small kernels and offline use. We introduce
\emph{super-analysis}, a middle ground that uses an SMT solver purely for
\emph{analysis} while transformations remain conventional, avoiding
synthesis entirely. The key observation is that the opportunities
conventional passes miss are typically not hard to \emph{transform} but hard
to \emph{justify}: proving a safety check dead can require facts from four
independent LLVM analyses \emph{simultaneously} --- a conjunctive entailment
problem suited to a solver, not to any single dataflow pass. We instantiate
super-analysis in \tool{}, an LLVM pass that proves sanitizer traps and
native-language safety checks unreachable and deletes them.
One mechanism serves three frontends and yields speedups in each:
\tool{} generates code up to $4.16\times$ faster than LLVM -O3 for a Julia
benchmark, speeds up a Swift SHA-256 kernel by \pct{8.8}, and delivers a
whole-library runtime win of \pct{2.6} for zstd compression in C. Latency
is a dial rather than a fixed cost:
\pct{96} of proofs survive a 100\,ms per-query SMT timeout budget, making
super-analysis viable as an online compiler pass rather than an offline
audit.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Compiler optimization research today occupies two distant extremes of a
cost--precision spectrum. At one end, conventional optimizers such as
LLVM's O3 pipeline~\cite{lattner2004llvm} make millions of decisions in
milliseconds, each justified by an ad-hoc heuristic or a lightweight
dataflow analysis; the heuristics are fast precisely because each one
reasons locally, over a single abstract domain, and gives up early. At the
other end, superoptimizers~\cite{massalin1987superoptimizer,
schkufza2013stoke, sasnauskas2017souper} hand the entire problem ---
analysis \emph{and} transformation --- to an expensive decision procedure
that searches for replacement code, achieving precision that heuristics
cannot match at costs that confine them to peephole windows and offline
runs.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{spectrum.png}
\caption{The cost--precision spectrum of aggressive compiler
optimization. Conventional pipelines are online but heuristic and
incomplete; superoptimizers are precise but offline. Super-analysis
occupies the unpopulated middle: on-demand SMT proofs at bounded latency,
with no synthesis, inside the compilation pipeline.}
\Description{A horizontal axis from online/low-latency on the left to
offline/high-latency on the right. Three boxes sit along it: Conventional
Optimization (LLVM -O3) on the left, Super-Analysis (ODeSSy) in the
middle, and Super-Optimization (Souper, STOKE) on the right.}
\label{fig:spectrum}
\end{figure}

This paper argues for a middle ground we call \emph{super-analysis}
(Figure~\ref{fig:spectrum}): use
the decision procedure for \emph{analysis only}, and leave transformation
to the conventional machinery that already exists. No code is synthesized;
no search is performed. The solver answers exactly one kind of question
--- \emph{is this program point reachable under this context?} --- and a
standard, trivially correct transformation (deleting an unreachable branch)
consumes the answer. Soundness is preserved by construction: an
\textsc{unsat} verdict is a proof, a \textsc{sat} or \textsc{unknown}
verdict changes nothing.
Why should a solver find opportunities that forty years of heuristics
miss? Our central empirical finding is that the missed opportunities are
typically not hard to \emph{transform} but hard to \emph{justify}. Proving
one bounds check dead in a real SHA-256 kernel requires the return-value
range of one function, a scalar-evolution loop bound, a value-range fact
on a third value, and two dominating branch conditions ---
\emph{simultaneously}. Each fact lives in a different LLVM analysis; no
single pass computes their conjunction, and the phase-ordering problem
ensures no fixed pipeline ever will. Deciding satisfiability of a
conjunction across abstract domains is, on principle, an entailment
problem --- exactly what an SMT solver decides and exactly what a
heuristic cannot. Our tool extracts the unsat core of every proof, so this
claim is not an intuition but a measurement: the cores name the
contributing analyses, and the majority of our nontrivial proofs draw on
two to four sources at once (\S\ref{sec:results-static}).
We instantiate super-analysis in \tool{}\footnote{On-Demand SMT System.},
an LLVM module pass targeting the most consequential class of
provably-dead code in modern software: \emph{safety checks}. Sanitizers
such as UBSan~\cite{ubsanDocs} and the native checks of memory-safe
languages (Swift bounds and overflow checks, Rust panics, Julia bounds
errors) compile to trap-guarded branches whose hot-path cost is the
standing tax of memory safety --- we measure it at \pct{4.9}--\pct{5.3} on
fully sanitized zlib and up to $4$--$5\times$ on native-language kernels.
Today the only recourse on hot paths is to turn checks \emph{off}
wholesale (\texttt{-Ounchecked}, \texttt{--check-bounds=no}), trading away
the safety guarantee entirely. \tool{} offers the sound alternative:
delete exactly the checks that are provably redundant with the guards,
contracts, and invariants already present in the program, and keep every
check that is not --- each deletion certified by an unsat core, audited
for vacuity, and validated by byte-identical program output.

Super-analysis is a general compilation paradigm rather than a single
optimization, and it carries one open problem we deliberately defer:
\emph{when is expensive analysis worth invoking?} A solver consulted
indiscriminately at every program point would be ruinous, and deciding
where precision repays its cost demands a cost model no compiler has.
Safety-check elimination is the ideal first instantiation precisely
because it answers that question for free. The work list is given --- run
exactly where the traps are, a set the frontend already enumerates. The
payoff needs no cost model --- erasing a check is unambiguously an
improvement in both code size and executed instructions, never a
trade-off to be weighed. And the transformation is trivial: folding a
branch whose condition is provably false is a rewrite every compiler
already performs correctly. Only the \emph{proof} is hard. This
application therefore isolates the paradigm's contribution --- precision
of analysis --- from the question it defers, and we return in
\S\ref{sec:future} to what invoking super-analysis elsewhere would
require.

This paper makes the following contributions:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Super-analysis} (\S\ref{sec:superanalysis}): a paradigm
  positioning between heuristic optimization and superoptimization ---
  SMT for analysis, conventional code for transformation.
\item \textbf{\tool{}} (\S\ref{sec:system}): a three-stage LLVM pass
  (serial discovery, parallel solve, serial transform) that eliminates sanitizer checks and optimizes the resulting IR. ODeSSy supports C/C++, Swift, and Julia as frontends and can enable transformations and speedups.
\item \textbf{An honest evaluation} (\S\ref{sec:results}): across C
  libraries (zlib, zstd, lz4, OpenSSL) and two native-check languages,
  \tool{} removes \pct{11}--\pct{13.6} of checks on real repositories;
  runtime experiments with output-equivalence gates,
  and 30-repetition medians on two ISAs show \emph{when} elimination pays
  --- a single hot loop proof in a swift kernel can buy \pct{3.7}, while 183 cold
  proofs in the entire crypto swift buy nothing.
\item \textbf{Latency as a dial} (\S\ref{sec:results-cost}): \pct{96} of
  zlib's proofs survive a 100\,ms per-query budget; proofs are generally cheap. The OpenSSL library's 
  long guard-chain queries need 30\,s, which makes ODeSSy a static analyzer for this benchmark.
\item \textbf{The formal frontier} (\S\ref{sec:future}): a taxonomy of
  residual checks from a three-language triage, and a formalization of
  four obligations (O1--O4) --- loop invariants, allocation contracts,
  frame conditions, cross-object transfer --- that separate present
  super-analysis from a measured $3.4\times$ ceiling behind heap
  invariants.
\item \textbf{Crossing the frontier} (\S\ref{sec:frame}):
  \emph{heap-invariant super-analysis} --- a FRAME fact source that
  discharges the frame condition (O3) with one MemorySSA walk per load
  pair, a definitional encoding that replaces free variables with the
  axioms of their defining code (retiring, along the way, the
  \texttt{freeze} instructions behind which the vectorizer had hidden
  \pct{81} of GEMM's trap conditions), and a subtraction-form trip-count
  fact whose upper bound is wrap-safe by construction. Together they take
  Julia's stdlib GEMM from 0 to 16-of-16 checks proven --- one composite
  proof draws on \emph{six} fact sources --- and proof-licensed
  \texttt{@inbounds} recovers \pct{96}--\pct{100} of the
  $3.6$--$4.2\times$ ceiling on both ISAs.
\end{itemize}

\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Sanitizers and native checks.}
Compiler-inserted safety checks come from two sources. Sanitizers
instrument C/C++ with checks for undefined or suspicious behavior ---
signed overflow, shifts, array bounds~\cite{serebryany2012asan,
ubsanDocs}; compiled with \texttt{-fsanitize-trap}, each check lowers to a
compare, a branch, and a trapping instruction. Memory-safe languages
insert equivalent checks natively: Swift traps on overflow and
out-of-bounds access by default, Rust panics on bounds violations
unconditionally in release builds, Julia bounds-checks array accesses
under its JIT. In both worlds the checks are semantically identical IR
patterns --- a conditional branch to a diverging block --- which is what
lets one tool serve both (\S\ref{sec:system}).
\paragraph{Superoptimization.}
From Massalin's exhaustive search~\cite{massalin1987superoptimizer}
through stochastic search~\cite{schkufza2013stoke} to Souper's
SMT-verified peepholes over LLVM IR~\cite{sasnauskas2017souper},
superoptimizers couple a candidate generator with a verifier and
\emph{synthesize} replacement code. Souper is the closest relative to this
work: it also queries an SMT solver about LLVM IR. The difference is
architectural: Souper's solver validates synthesized candidates over
bounded peephole windows, whereas \tool{} performs no synthesis at all ---
its solver decides reachability over arbitrarily structured acyclic
slices, and the ``transformation'' is branch folding that LLVM has
performed correctly for decades. This is why super-analysis scales to
whole libraries (156k-line IR modules; 12{,}798 checks in zstd) that no
synthesizing tool can process, while remaining, like Souper, a
capability-and-honest-accounting story rather than a raw-speedup one.
\paragraph{Solver-aided compilation and validation.}
Equality saturation~\cite{tate2009equality} replaces destructive phase
ordering with a saturating rewrite engine; it shares our diagnosis
(heuristic pipelines miss conjunctions of facts) but attacks the
transformation side. Alive2~\cite{lopes2021alive2} uses SMT to
\emph{validate} existing LLVM transformations; \tool{} inverts the
direction, using SMT to \emph{enable} transformations that the pipeline
could not itself justify. Work testing LLVM's dataflow analyses against
SMT ground truth~\cite{taneja2020knownbits} quantifies the precision gap
we exploit; our fact-import tier (\S\ref{sec:system-facts}) closes the
loop by asserting those same analyses' results \emph{into} the solver, so
the proof combines the strengths of both.
\paragraph{Check-elimination and polyhedral techniques.}
Classic bounds-check elimination in JITs and ahead-of-time compilers is
heuristic and local; polyhedral frameworks~\cite{grosser2012polly} decide
affine questions exactly but are confined to affine loops --- our
opportunity matrix confirms LLVM already handles the affine case
(PolyBench emits zero residual traps at O3) while failing on the
data-dependent loops of compression and cryptography, which is precisely
where \tool{} operates. Recent empirical work questioning marginal alias
precision~\cite{relaxingalias2025} supports our strategy of spending
solver time only at check sites --- the places where precision provably
monetizes.

\section{Super-Analysis}
\label{sec:superanalysis}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{super-opt_cost.png}\\[0.9em]
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{super-analysis_cost.png}
\caption{Where the cost goes. \emph{Top:} super-optimization pays twice
--- an expensive search over candidate programs, plus an SMT equivalence
check \emph{per candidate}. \emph{Bottom:} super-analysis drops the search
entirely; the transformation is a rewrite the compiler already knows, and
the solver decides one fact per site rather than validating a stream of
guesses. \textbf{Super-optimization is super-expensive because of synthesis;
super-analysis is merely expensive.}}
\Description{Two stacked flow diagrams. The top diagram shows
Super-Optimization branching into Synthesis (marked triple-dollar) and SMT
equivalence checking (double-dollar). The bottom diagram shows
Super-Analysis branching into rules-based transformation (marked one cent)
and SMT fact proof (double-dollar).}
\label{fig:cost}
\end{figure}

\subsection{The spectrum}
Conventional optimization and superoptimization differ in \emph{which
component} pays for precision. A conventional pass has cheap analysis and
cheap transformation, and both are heuristic. A superoptimizer makes both
expensive: the search proposes code, the solver verifies it. Super-analysis
splits the difference asymmetrically: expensive, precise, \emph{sound}
analysis; cheap, conventional, trivially correct transformation. The
design wagers that in mature pipelines the transformations are not the
bottleneck --- LLVM already knows how to fold a branch, unroll a loop, and
vectorize a body --- and that what is missing is the \emph{license} to do
so.

Figure~\ref{fig:cost} makes the cost consequence concrete. A
superoptimizer's dominant expense is not the solver but the
\emph{synthesis loop} that feeds it: a search over candidate programs,
each candidate requiring its own equivalence query. Super-analysis deletes
that outer loop. What remains is one decision procedure invocation per
check site --- a decision, not a search --- and a transformation the
compiler performs for free. This is why the two paradigms differ by orders
of magnitude in latency while sharing the same underlying technology, and
why super-analysis can run inside the pipeline rather than beside it
(\S\ref{sec:results-cost}).

\subsection{Why conjunction necessitates a solver}
\label{sec:conjunction}
Consider the message-schedule loop of a Swift SHA-256 kernel, whose inner
16 iterations index a 64-element working array. Swift's bounds checks on
these accesses survive both \texttt{swiftc -O} and a subsequent full LLVM
O3. \tool{} proves them dead, and the extracted unsat cores show why no
single heuristic could. Each proof's minimal core has the shape:
\[
\textsf{Spec} \wedge \textsf{RM} \wedge \textsf{SCEV} \wedge \textsf{LVI}
\wedge \textsf{SCEV}' \wedge G_0 \wedge G_k \;\vdash\; \bot
\]
--- a return-value range (\textsf{RM}), two scalar-evolution facts
(\textsf{SCEV}), a lattice value range (\textsf{LVI}), and two dominating
guard conditions ($G_0$, $G_k$), jointly inconsistent with the check
firing. Four independent analyses and path conditions must be intersected;
each in isolation is insufficient (removing any core member returns
\textsc{sat}). LLVM's analyses do not compose this way: each consumes the
IR and produces facts in its own domain, and no pass computes the
satisfiability of their conjunction --- nor could a fixed pipeline, since
which facts matter is different at every check site. Deciding such
conjunctions is the definitional competence of an SMT
solver~\cite{demoura2008z3}. This is the sense in which super-analysis is
\emph{necessary} rather than merely convenient: the reasoning is
entailment over a conjunction of theories, not dataflow over a lattice.
\subsection{Irreducibility, stated honestly}
\label{sec:irreducibility}
The dual claim bounds what any tool can do. If no conjunction of program
facts implies a check's redundancy, then \emph{no sound tool may remove
it}: the check is not overhead but specification. \tool{} monetizes
\emph{redundancy} between the guards, contracts, and invariants a program
already contains and the checks a compiler inserts; where that redundancy
is zero, all-\textsc{sat} is the correct output. Our evaluation treats
such results as findings, not failures: crypto code whose unsigned
wraparound \emph{is} the algorithm produces all-\textsc{sat} under the
unsigned specification (\S\ref{sec:results-static}), and Julia's own
compiler strands a measured $3.4\times$ behind checks whose redundancy
requires heap reasoning outside today's fact sources
(\S\ref{sec:future}).

\section{\tool{}: Design and Implementation}
\label{sec:system}
\tool{} is an LLVM module pass of roughly six thousand lines, organized as
a three-stage pipeline whose structure is dictated by two requirements:
soundness under aggressive parallelism, and bit-reproducibility of
verdicts regardless of thread count.
\subsection{Stage 1: discovery (serial)}
A \emph{hunter} enumerates trap sites: calls to
\texttt{llvm.ubsantrap}/\texttt{llvm.trap}, plus --- behind a divergence
gate that requires the callee to be no-return or followed by
\texttt{unreachable} --- any callee named in the \texttt{traps=} pass
parameter. This one mechanism retargets the pass to Swift runtime checks,
Rust's \texttt{core::panicking} family (\texttt{panic=abort} builds), and
Julia's outlined \texttt{jl\_bounds\_error}/\texttt{boundserror} thunks
with zero per-language code. An \emph{anchor} then creates one job per
\emph{incoming edge} of each trap block, enabling partial elimination of
error blocks shared by many checks. For each job, discovery collects the
dominating branch conditions (the guard context), harvests
\texttt{llvm.assume} facts, and computes a backward slice whose boundary
values --- loop-header phis, loads, and calls whose semantics we do not
encode --- become free variables. Free variables are the soundness
mechanism: anything not understood is over-approximated by an
unconstrained symbol, so \textsc{unsat} verdicts hold for \emph{every}
concrete behavior of the unencoded parts.
\subsection{Stage 2: solve (parallel)}
Workers claim jobs in discovery-index order. Each job owns a private Z3
context (contexts are not thread-safe; disjoint contexts on disjoint
threads are), encodes its slice in reverse post-order --- arithmetic, the
full overflow-intrinsic family, shifts, spaghetti control flow with
memoized CFG-reachability encoding --- asserts the guard context, and asks
whether the trap condition is satisfiable. \textsc{unsat} marks the trap
provably dead; \textsc{sat} and \textsc{unknown} (solver timeout) leave it
untouched. Every assertion is tracked, so an \textsc{unsat} verdict yields
a minimal core naming exactly which guards and facts proved the check
dead.
\subsubsection{Fact tiers}
\label{sec:system-facts}
The \emph{light} tier constrains free variables only by their IR types.
The \emph{heavy} tier additionally imports LLVM's own conclusions as
solver constraints at the over-approximation boundary: \texttt{!range}
metadata (RM), KnownBits (KB), LazyValueInfo ranges (LVI), scalar
evolution ranges (SCEV), and a symbolic trip-count translation (SCEVSYM)
that renders loop bounds --- including an exact unsigned-division
translation and a stride gate behind SCEV's no-wrap facts --- as
constraints on boundary phis. Each fact is audit-labeled, so cores
attribute proofs to their sources; the sha256 cores of
\S\ref{sec:conjunction} are heavy-tier cores. Because LVI and SCEV memoize
internally (an unsynchronized data race, and worse, a potential source of
corrupted facts), all heavy-tier queries pass through a \emph{FactGate}: a
ticket turnstile keyed on discovery index that serializes analysis queries
in a canonical order. One mechanism thus provides thread safety \emph{and}
determinism: verdicts, output IR, and logs are identical for
\texttt{threads=1} and \texttt{threads=N}, a property we verify by
\texttt{diff} and offer as a reproducibility artifact.
\subsubsection{The vacuity audit}
A wrong ``proof'' is the one failure mode this tool must not have. If the
encoded context is itself inconsistent --- through an encoder bug or a
genuinely contradictory guard chain --- every trap under it is trivially
\textsc{unsat}. Under the \texttt{vacuity} option \tool{} re-checks each
context alone; if the context is unsatisfiable without the trap condition,
the elimination is \emph{refused} and reported. The audit is deliberately
self-distrusting: on zstd it refused 61 eliminations on xxHash whose
guard chains are jointly unsatisfiable for the actual constants ---
likely sound eliminations, conservatively surrendered because the tool
cannot distinguish that situation from its own bugs. We report vacuous
counts in every experiment; they are zero except where noted.
\subsection{Stage 3: transform (serial)}
After the pool joins, the main thread folds the proven branches in
discovery order (the only IR mutation in the pass, with a double-fold
guard), and standard \texttt{simplifycfg} and \texttt{adce} reclaim the
dead blocks. Verdicts are computed on pristine IR; all mutation is
deferred. Workers fence all exceptions and degrade to ``keep the trap'' ---
the universal failure posture: every error path in the system converges on
declining to optimize.

\section{Experimental Methodology}
\label{sec:method}
\paragraph{Machines.}
x86 measurements use a CloudLab~\cite{cloudlab} \texttt{c220g2} node:
dual Xeon E5-2660~v3 (Haswell-EP, 10 cores/socket, AVX2, no AVX-512),
DDR4-2133, Ubuntu 24.04, with Intel turbo disabled and every timed run
pinned to socket~0 (\texttt{numactl}). Apple-Silicon measurements use an
M-series MacBook Pro. The pass builds against pinned LLVM trunk
(23.0.0git) with Z3 4.8.12; Swift 6.3.3, Rust 1.97, Julia 1.12.
\paragraph{Timing protocol.}
Corpora live in tmpfs; every configuration is warmed; repetitions are
\emph{shuffled and interleaved} so drift spreads uniformly across
configurations; finals use 30 repetitions (20 for whole-library C runs)
and \emph{medians} are primary. Two controls isolate attribution: a
\texttt{base} binary (one verify round-trip through the identical
emit-IR/\texttt{opt}/\texttt{llc} sandwich) and a \texttt{base2x} binary
(the same full O3 applied \emph{twice}), so oracle-vs-\texttt{base2x}
deltas cannot be an artifact of extra conventional optimization exposure.
The \texttt{base}--\texttt{base2x} gap doubles as a per-run noise floor.
\paragraph{Honesty gates.}
Before any timing is trusted, all binaries must produce byte-identical
output on the workload; a mismatch aborts the experiment. Static
``eliminated'' counts are recomputed from the final IR, never inferred
from verdicts; crashes and timeouts are marked, never counted as
eliminations. Per-query solver timeout is 300\,ms in performance builds
(10\,s in library audits); \S\ref{sec:results-cost} shows the provable set
is \pct{96} saturated at 100\,ms on the densest workload, and the
performance builds' per-platform elimination counts exactly match the
audit runs', so no proof was lost to the budget.
\paragraph{Research questions.}
Our evaluation is organized around three questions, one per contribution
that admits measurement:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{RQ1a --- How much can super-analysis prove?}
  Static elimination across C libraries and three native-check languages,
  with the unsat cores that attribute each proof to its fact sources
  (\S\ref{sec:results-static}).
\item \textbf{RQ1b --- When do proofs become speed?}
  Checked-vs-unchecked ceilings, the fraction of each ceiling recovered,
  and the dose--response account relating \emph{where} a proof lands to
  \emph{whether} it pays (\S\ref{sec:results-perf}).
\item \textbf{RQ2 --- What does it cost?}
  Compile latency, parallel scaling, and the per-query timeout dial that
  makes super-analysis viable in-pipeline (\S\ref{sec:results-cost}).
\end{itemize}

unsigned from 41 to 25 --- the solver's exactness through shift
compositions is doing attributable work that range/known-bits reasoning
loses.

the final encoder's two additional proofs nearly close the ceiling.) The adler32

buy nothing, one hot proof in adler32 buys \pct{3.7}.

Appendix~\ref{sec:app-full}) is equally load-bearing. Where eliminations do not
unlock a transformation, re-optimization is a genuine two-sided lottery:
the same adler32 elimination that gains \pct{3.7} on Haswell costs
\pct{1.5} on M-series, and sha1 on x86 --- where Linux code generation
keeps only 2 of the 7 proofs on the hot path --- \emph{loses}
\pct{5}, a result we replicated in four independent 30-rep runs
spanning a machine reboot and an encoder revision ($-4.5$, $-4.65$,
$-4.83$, $-5.02$). We report this as a
first-class finding: the value of a check is non-uniform in both
magnitude and \emph{sign}, and microarchitecture is part of the
experiment. This is also why ceilings matter methodologically: reporting
recovery as a fraction of each machine's own checked-vs-unchecked ceiling
makes results portable across microarchitectures in a way raw percentages
are not.
Finally, whole-library C: \tool{} eliminates \pct{11} of zlib's checks
with runtime exactly flat against the double-O3 control across three
corpus sizes and three fact tiers --- while the library's measured
sanitizer overhead stands at \pct{5}. The proven checks are cold; the
overhead is concentrated in hot-loop checks whose redundancy, todays'
fact sources cannot establish (\S\ref{sec:future}). The eliminations do
pay in code size (\texttt{.text} shrinks \pct{0.5}--\pct{1.2} versus the
control) at zero runtime risk --- and the honest summary of the C story
is capability plus accounting, in the tradition of
Souper~\cite{sasnauskas2017souper}, not raw speedup. One C library
breaks the pattern: on zstd under the signed specification (the
unsigned one is un-runnable in trap mode, \S\ref{sec:results-static}),
\emph{six} full-tier eliminations buy \pct{2.3} on whole-file
compression against the double-O3 control with a \pct{0.24} noise
floor and byte-identical output --- the study's only whole-library C
speedup, and itself a confirmation of dose location: decompression,
whose signed checks cost \pct{9} when all present, stays exactly flat
because its surviving checks' redundancy is precisely what today's
fact sources cannot establish.

\section{Crossing the Frontier: Heap-Invariant Super-Analysis}
\label{sec:frame}
The taxonomy of \S\ref{sec:future} was written first and acted on
second: this section reports the discharge of its dominant class on its
own acceptance test. The target is the kernel of Julia's standard-library
GEMM (\texttt{generic\_matmatmul!}) with its \emph{real} dimension guards
restored --- the benchmark on which \tool{} as evaluated in
\S\ref{sec:results} proves \emph{zero} of 16 residual bounds-check edges
against a measured $3.4\times$ checked-vs-unchecked ceiling. Results in
this section use an extended encoder (three additions below); all
numbers in \S\ref{sec:results} were produced by the base encoder and are
unchanged --- both are pinned in the artifact.

\subsection{Three additions}
\label{sec:frame-machinery}
\paragraph{FRAME: the frame rule as a fact source.}
The frame gap has a one-line statement: the guard constrains one load of
\texttt{size(A,1)}, the check consumes a \emph{different} load of the
same address, and nothing connects them. The connecting theorem is the
separation-logic frame rule~\cite{reynolds2002separation}: if no memory
write between loads $L_1$ and $L_2$ of the same pointer can touch that
location, the loads agree. We discharge the premise with a single
MemorySSA walk per candidate pair, asking for the clobbering access of
$L_2$ \emph{at $L_1$'s own memory location} --- address, width, and
aliasing metadata. Querying with $L_1$'s location is the load-bearing
choice: the check-side loads are vectorizer-cloned and carry no metadata
at all (which is precisely why LLVM itself leaves the checks in), while
the guard-side load carries Julia's scoped no-alias metadata, under which
stock \textsc{ScopedNoAliasAA} proves every loop-carried store harmless.
If the walk returns $L_1$'s defining access (or one dominating it), the
equality $L_1 = L_2$ enters the query as a fact labeled
$|\mathrm{FRAME}{:}k|$ --- context-side, vacuity-audited, and guarded by
two standing \textsc{sat} tripwire tests (a may-alias clobber and a
single-arm clobber must refuse forever). No new alias analysis is
written: the walk delegates every disjointness verdict to LLVM's
existing stack and \emph{refuses} on anything unmodeled, the same
posture as every other import in \S\ref{sec:system-facts}.
\paragraph{Definitional encoding of stabilized values.}
Three successive countermodel inspections --- our standing diagnostic:
a should-prove query returning \textsc{sat} in microseconds indicates a
\emph{missing constraint}, not solver hardness --- exposed a chain of
encoding gaps, each closed by the same principle: replace a free
variable with the \emph{definitional axiom} of its defining code.
First, \texttt{freeze}: the vectorizer's multiversioning stabilizes its
guard flags against poison, and 13 of GEMM's 16 trap conditions were
freeze-wrapped --- encoded as unconstrained Booleans, their queries were
trivially satisfiable no matter what facts we supplied. Since
\texttt{freeze} is the identity on every non-poison operand, encoding
$\mathit{freeze}(x) = x$ is sound over the encoder's
over-approximation (a poisoned operand is itself a free variable and can
assume the frozen choice). Second, trip-count bounds moved to
\emph{subtraction form}: asserting $\phi - \mathit{start} \le_u
s{\cdot}\mathrm{BTC}$ instead of $\phi \le_u \mathit{start} +
s{\cdot}\mathrm{BTC}$ makes the unit-stride upper bound unconditionally
wrap-safe --- modular subtraction cancels overflow exactly --- which
retires the no-wrap side conditions and admits the \emph{symbolic}
starts of remainder loops for free. Third, the leaves of those bound
expressions (the vectorizer's resume indices and the preamble computing
them) are \emph{pre-encoded} through the ordinary instruction encoder:
selects become if-then-elses, merge-point phis become unions guarded by
their encoded branch predicates. No ``trip count $>0$'' is asserted
anywhere; the countermodels claiming to stand inside zero-trip loops die
because they violate the definitions. Every addition is
true of all executions by construction, so the over-approximation ---
and hence soundness --- is untouched.

still buy nothing, because count is not value. A full-suite regression
sweep is strictly monotone --- no benchmark lost a proof, and the
vacuity audit reports zero on every one.

\section{Limitations and Future Work}
\label{sec:future}
\subsection{A taxonomy of the residue}
Triaging every surviving \textsc{sat} across Swift, Rust, and Julia yields
four classes: (a) \emph{symbolic trip counts} --- solved by SCEVSYM, the
source of the sha256 unlock; (b) \emph{heap length invariants}
(\texttt{array.count == n} across blocks and objects) --- the dominant
class, blocking gemm, matmul, nbody, and the Swift residuals; (c)
\emph{value-dependent overflow} --- irreducible without value
information, correctly kept; (d) \emph{non-unit strides} --- SCEV computes
no trip count; a separate induction argument is required. The residue is
not cheap: base64 --- whose stride-3 hot loop is class (d) and whose
runtime-built lookup table is class (b)'s allocation-carried case (the
6-bit-masked indices are provably in range; the table's \emph{length}
is an undischarged O2 allocation contract) --- runs \pct{56.8} faster
with checks off, the largest kernel ceiling we measured, entirely
unrecovered. Class (b) is
where the money is, and it is not an engineering gap but a missing
\emph{theory} in the fact sources --- the theory that
\S\ref{sec:frame} supplies for its guard-carried case. What remains of
(b) after \S\ref{sec:frame} is its \emph{allocation-carried} case:
Swift's global arrays (nbody) route every access through runtime calls
(\texttt{beginAccess}, uniqueness checks, reference counting) that
declare no memory effects, so every frame walk is --- correctly ---
refused; discharging them needs a small table of trusted runtime
mod/ref axioms plus, for copy-on-write, a \emph{preservation} invariant
(every write preserves the length) rather than a no-clobber one. Classes
(c) and (d) stand.
\subsection{The formal frontier: O1--O4}
The minimal program exhibiting class (b) is
\texttt{a := alloc(n); for i in 0..n-1: if i >= len(a): trap}. Its
verification condition, $0 \le i \le n{-}1 \wedge \mathrm{Inv}(H)
\Rightarrow i < \mathrm{len}(H, a)$, factors into four obligations:
\textbf{O1}, the loop invariant $0 \le i < n$ --- solved
(SCEV/SCEVSYM; it produced the sha256 result); \textbf{O2}, the
allocation contract $\mathrm{len}(\mathrm{alloc}(n)) = n$ --- a trusted
axiom of the runtime interface, object-layout knowledge rather than code
analysis; \textbf{O3}, the \emph{frame condition} --- no intervening store
aliases the length field, so two loads of it unify (the separation-logic
frame rule~\cite{reynolds2002separation}); and \textbf{O4}, cross-object
transfer ($\mathrm{len}(A) = \mathrm{len}(C)$), reachable through guards
once O3 moves them, shared allocation arguments once O2 lands, or caller
summaries.
This factorization was predictive, and the paper's own timeline tests
it. The frame gap is not hypothetical: we measured it (the zero row of
Table~\ref{tab:static}) --- Julia's own Base GEMM with its real
dimension guards restored, where the guard constrains one load of
\texttt{size(A,1)}, the check consumes a \emph{different} load, and
nothing connects them. \S\ref{sec:frame} then discharges O3 exactly as
prescribed here --- one MemorySSA walk per load pair, refusal on
anything unmodeled, tripwires before features --- and O4's guard-carried
case follows through the restored guards, closing GEMM completely. What
remains open is O2 (the allocation contract as a trusted per-runtime
axiom table) and O3's \emph{call-carried} case: Swift's runtime
functions declare no memory effects, so nbody's $+410$\% ceiling still
stands behind a mod/ref axiom table and, for copy-on-write, a
preservation-style invariant --- the successor program's remaining
work, with the same discipline: because a wrong frame fact is silent
unsoundness, the vacuity audit and \textsc{sat} tripwire tests of
\S\ref{sec:system} are preconditions, not afterthoughts.
\subsection{Beyond check elimination}
Nothing in super-analysis is specific to traps. The same
solve-then-transform discipline can serve range estimation, constant
generation, and flag inference inside existing passes --- an on-demand
oracle consulted where heuristics give up (single-threaded, at dialed-down
budgets, per \S\ref{sec:results-cost}). Moving beyond traps, however,
means confronting the question \S\ref{sec:intro} deferred: outside check
elimination the work list is not given, and the payoff is a trade-off
rather than an unambiguous win, so invoking the solver profitably requires
a cost model --- deciding \emph{where} precision repays its latency. We
regard that as the paradigm's central open problem.
Datalog-style pointer analyses
offer a cheaper carrier for the O3 alias obligations than raw SMT, and are
a natural hybrid. Farther out, languages that already carry invariants ---
Dafny-style contracts~\cite{leino2010dafny}, or annotations lowered to
LLVM IR --- would let super-analysis consume \emph{stated} facts rather
than inferred ones, turning verification effort directly into
optimization. Engineering limitations we state plainly: Julia's JIT
accepts no external IR --- its \texttt{code\_llvm} dumps thread
session-absolute runtime addresses and GC state through a Julia-specific
convention, so they are analyzable but not executable --- hence our
Julia runtime path is proof-licensed annotation (\S\ref{sec:frame}),
which we regard as a deployment model rather than a workaround; the
pass is intraprocedural
(aggressive inlining is our interprocedural stopgap); and OpenSSL-class
guard chains motivate query splitting or context pruning.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Super-analysis occupies a deliberately asymmetric point between heuristic
optimization and superoptimization: a solver where precision is the
bottleneck, conventional machinery everywhere else. Instantiated in
\tool{}, the paradigm proves \pct{11}--\pct{13.6} of sanitizer checks dead
across real C libraries and 215 checks in an unmodified Swift library ---
every deletion certified by an unsat core, audited for vacuity, and
validated by byte-identical output --- and, where proofs gate
transformations, converts them into \pct{3.7}--\pct{8.8} speedups on two
ISAs atop fully optimized baselines, recovering up to \pct{98} of the
unsound checks-off ceiling. The evaluation's shape is itself a contribution: a
dose--response account of when elimination pays, a replicated
demonstration that it can also cost, and a latency dial spanning five
orders of magnitude of query hardness. The unsat cores certify the
paradigm's premise --- the missing reasoning is conjunctive across
analysis domains, hence a solver's problem --- and the O1--O4 obligations
chart what stands between today's tool and the $3.4\times$ ceiling behind
heap invariants. That chart, drawn first as future work, proved
predictive within the paper itself: a frame-rule fact source and a
definitional encoding discharge O3 exactly as prescribed, prove all 16
residual checks in Julia's own stdlib GEMM, and recover
\pct{96}--\pct{100} of the $3.6$--$4.2\times$ ceiling on both ISAs
through proof-licensed annotation. The performance cost of memory safety
is not a fixed tax; it is a provably reducible one, and super-analysis
is a scalable way to reduce it.

\section*{Ethics and Privacy Statement}
This work removes only program checks that are formally proven unreachable,
preserving all safety guarantees of the original binaries; every
elimination carries a machine-checkable certificate and our tool refuses
proofs it cannot audit. A dual-use consideration is that reachability
analysis of safety checks could, in principle, help locate reachable
(buggy) checks; this information is equally available to standard fuzzing
and static analysis, and our tool neither exploits nor reports it beyond
keeping such checks intact. No human subjects or personal data are
involved.
